## Q2. What does a typical high-performing slide sequence look like, how many slides, and how much text per slide?

### Takeaway
The evidence converges on these points:
- **Length:** 8 to 12 slides.
- **Hook:** an outcome-led, specific cover.
- **Pacing:** one idea per slide.
- **Ending:** a single CTA on the last slide.

On Instagram, engagement dips after slide 3, then recovers at 8 or more slides, peaking at full-length carousels. On LinkedIn, 9 to 12-slide decks beat 6 to 8-slide decks. Text should be minimal: a 6 to 8-word hook, one headline plus one subhead per slide, and roughly 20% or less of the slide as text.

### Cited Findings
- Socialinsider's study of about 22M Instagram posts (data around 2020): most carousels have 2 to 4 slides and only 6% use the then-maximum of 10. Engagement drops after 3 slides and climbs again from 8 slides; 10-slide carousels had the highest engagement, above 2% — [Socialinsider PDF](https://www.socialinsider.io/data-geeks/Instagram-Carousel-Study.pdf); [SEJ coverage](https://searchenginejournal.com/instagram-carousels/379311).
- The same study found that mixed image and video carousels had the highest engagement (2.33%), and that writing "swipe left" in the caption raised average engagement from 1.83% to 2% — [Socialinsider PDF](https://www.socialinsider.io/data-geeks/Instagram-Carousel-Study.pdf).
- Instagram raised the carousel limit to 20 slides (August 2024). Recommended: 8 to 10 slides at 1080×1350 (4:5), with text at 20% or less of each slide — [PostEverywhere](https://posteverywhere.ai/blog/instagram-carousel-best-practices).
- LinkedIn (article dated 31 August 2026): native document carousels average 6.90% engagement, against 5.00% for images. In MagicPost's 2026 analysis of 67,765 carousels, 9 to 12-slide posts had a median of 49 likes, against 26 for 6 to 8 slides. Recommended: 8 to 12 slides, one idea each, a 4:5 portrait format, and one CTA after the value is delivered — [Socialinsider LinkedIn](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/linkedin-carousel/).
- Hook slide: 6 to 8 words that highlight the payoff, with a visual cue such as an arrow or underline; proof (testimonial or metrics) on the second-to-last slide — [Postiv](https://postiv.ai/blog/linkedin-carousel-best-practices).
- Common structure is problem, then value slides, then CTA — [Expandi](https://expandi.io/?p=12955). LinkedIn image carousels are no longer native, so upload them as a PDF — [Draftly](https://draftly.substack.com/p/linkedin-carousels-create-and-optimize).
- One headline and one subhead per slide; design at 4:5 — [PostNitro](https://postnitro.ai/blog/post/instagram-carousel-design).
- Slide 1 should name a specific question ("Which air purifier size fits this room?", not "Everything you need to know about…"). Slide 2 names the tradeoff and gives a short roadmap. The example flow is 8 slides; reusable flow types are a decision checklist (5 stages), comparison (4), expert explanation (4) and proof breakdown (5) — [TrueFuture Media](https://www.truefuturemedia.com/articles/instagram-carousel-strategy-2026).

### Inferences
- A suggested template for an AI-tool carousel, built from the sources above (8 to 10 slides):
  1. Hook: a specific outcome plus a visual cue
  2. Problem or context
  3. Tool reveal: logo plus a real UI screenshot
  4–7. One feature or step per slide, each with a screenshot
  8. Comparison or proof
  9. Recap or pricing
  10. A single CTA: save, follow, or link in bio
- For news carousels, 5 to 8 slides is likely enough (headline, what happened, why it matters, details, reaction, CTA). The engagement dip after slide 3 means slides 2 and 3 must carry strong curiosity.

### Gaps
- No 2025-2026 Instagram-specific slide-count study was found. The Socialinsider Instagram data is about 2020, from before the 20-slide limit.
- No quantified words-per-slide study. "20% text" and "6 to 8-word hook" are vendor rules of thumb.

## Q3. What visual patterns recur (dark vs light, colors, fonts, screenshots and mockups, big numbers, headline cards, swipe cues, branding)?

### Takeaway
The sourced guidance stresses consistency over novelty:
- the same layouts, margins, type scale, colors and navigation cues on every slide
- large type with strong contrast
- a 4:5 portrait format
- real product or UI imagery shown when it backs a claim

Dark mode, glassmorphism and clean typography are named as the AI/SaaS carousel style by at least one commercial designer. Dark versus light performance was not quantified anywhere.

### Cited Findings
- Reuse layouts, margins, type scale, colors and navigation cues on every slide; use large type, strong contrast and short text blocks; preview on mobile — [Socialinsider LinkedIn](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/linkedin-carousel/).
- Show the actual product, process or document when it supports a claim. Don't rely only on color to carry information, and keep ratios consistent — [TrueFuture Media](https://www.truefuturemedia.com/articles/instagram-carousel-strategy-2026).
- A Behance carousel designer for AI and SaaS says they prioritize "dark mode, glassmorphism, and clean typography" — [Behance carousel tag](https://www.behance.net/tags/carousel).
- Template marketplaces (Venngage, Visme, Adobe Stock) offer "Tech & AI trends" carousel templates — [Venngage](https://venngage.com/templates/social-media/4-tech--ai-trends-portrait-instagram-carousel-4d5304cc-7db3-4367-8623-4fdafdd45b07); [Visme](https://www.visme.co/templates/social-media-graphics/tech-trends-instagram-carousel-post-templates-1425286788/).
- Tweet-screenshot carousels: capture the tweet, crop tightly, add padding and a background, and use 4:5. Multi-tweet carousels are called "one of the highest-performing formats" (an unverified vendor claim) — [TwitterShots](https://twittershots.com/blog/how-to-turn-tweet-into-Instagram-carousel); [Tweet Archivist](https://www.tweetarchivist.com/how-to-share-tweets-on-instagram). Tools such as Contentdrips turn each tweet in a thread into its own branded slide — [Contentdrips](https://contentdrips.com/features/twitter-carousel-generator/).
- The "card news" format, a designed series of square text cards, is a distinct style from raw screenshots — [tessl card-news-generator](https://tessl.io/registry/skills/github/bear2u/my-skills/card-news-generator/quality).

### Inferences
- For an AI and tech brand, a defensible system is:
  - **Background and accent:** a dark base (near-black or deep navy) with one saturated accent color for keywords and numbers.
  - **Type:** one heavy sans-serif for headlines and one neutral sans-serif for body text.
  - **Branding:** a fixed header or footer strip carrying the handle, slide counter and logo.
  - **Navigation:** a consistent "swipe" arrow on slides 1 to N-1.
  - **Product proof:** real screenshots in a simple device or browser frame.
  This follows from the consistency, contrast and real-product guidance above. The dark-mode preference rests on one designer's statement plus an unverified convention.
- Big-number slides ("10x faster", "$0") and headline or tweet cards are the natural fit for news and tool-reveal slides. No performance data isolates them.

### Gaps
- No A/B data on dark versus light backgrounds, specific fonts, or device mockups versus flat screenshots.
- No verified teardown of the visual systems of @evolving.ai, Rundown, Superhuman, TechCrunch, The Verge or Product Hunt.

## Q4. Which recurring formats perform ("5 AI tools you didn't know", X vs Y, news breakdown, tutorial, tweet screenshots)?

### Takeaway
The formats observed among exemplar accounts are tool breakdowns or listicles, side-by-side comparisons, "1 tool = 1 idea" posts, simple explainers of AI terms, daily micro-lessons, visual AI news, and tweet or thread screenshot roundups. Only anecdotal or vendor evidence ties any of them to higher performance.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- A sensible rotation for a Turkish AI and tech account:
  - tool listicles ("Bilmediğin 5 yapay zeka aracı")
  - "X vs Y" comparisons (ChatGPT vs Gemini)
  - news breakdowns (headline card, then 3 to 5 explainer slides)
  - step-by-step tutorials with screenshots
  - tweet or quote cards for reactions

## Q5. What do carousel designers and educators say works, and which mistakes make carousels look cheap or AI-generated?

### Takeaway
Practitioners agree on what works:
- a specific cover
- one job per slide
- consistent branding
- readable type
- real product proof
- one CTA

Cheapness comes from:
- vague covers and tiny text
- inconsistent styling and template overload
- decorative clutter
- wrong cropping between platforms
- pitching on every slide

### Cited Findings
- The main mistakes are vague covers, tiny text, poor flow and sloppy branding; inconsistent styling looks "disjointed and unprofessional" — [PostNitro mistakes](https://postnitro.ai/blog/post/carousel-design-mistakes); [PostNitro 2024 list](https://postnitro.ai/blog/post/avoid-these-10-carousel-design-mistakes-in-2024).
- The wrong template can make a tip look like a sales pitch or cram detail into tiny text. Overlapping shapes and unidentifiable decorative elements reduce clarity. A one-size-fits-all export causes awkward crops and cut-off text — [Decktopus vs Canva](https://www.decktopus.com/blog/decktopus-vs-canva-carousels); [Gamma](https://gamma.app/de/explore/content/guides/what-ai-tools-create-professional-social-media-carousels).
- Further mistakes: claims beyond the evidence, stating a demo's result without its limits, pitching on every slide, a CTA that doesn't match the cover's question, and treating saves as conversions — [TrueFuture Media](https://www.truefuturemedia.com/articles/instagram-carousel-strategy-2026).
- Socialinsider frames its rules as hypotheses: test one variable at a time and judge by swipe-through, completion and save rates — [Socialinsider LinkedIn](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/linkedin-carousel/).

### Inferences
- For an automated or AI-assisted pipeline, the likely "AI-generated" tells are:
  - generic stock or AI images in place of real UI screenshots
  - inconsistent margins and type sizes between slides
  - overlong text blocks
  - emoji-heavy bullets
  - default template decoration
  These follow from the sourced mistakes. Enforce a locked grid, a type scale and real product captures.

### Gaps
- No named LinkedIn carousel ghostwriter or educator (for example Justin Welsh or Jasmin Alić) was found quoted with first-hand data. Results were mostly vendor blogs.

## Q6. What should be considered for Turkish-language carousels?

### Takeaway
Turkish copy runs somewhat longer than English: about 5 to 15% for short marketing captions, and up to about 33% in vendor estimates. Individual words get very long because of suffixes, so the main risk is long words overflowing narrow text boxes. Uppercasing needs Turkish-aware handling of i/İ and ı/I.

### Cited Findings
- Turkish can expand by up to about 33% compared with English (translation vendor) — [Andovar](https://dev.andovar.com/turkish-translation-services).
- Turkish app-store captions run 5 to 15% longer than English — [App Screenshot Studio](https://appscreenshotstudio.com/turkish-app-store-screenshots).
- Turkish builds words by stacking suffixes onto a root, so single words get long and break narrow slots. Uppercasing must use locale-aware functions because of the dotted and dotless i — [skills.sh localize-tr](https://www.skills.sh/i18n-agent/i18nstack/localize-tr).
- Short strings grow more in translation than long ones: about 100% for 1 to 4 characters, about 10% above 50 characters — [Microsoft VB6 guidance (old)](https://learn.microsoft.com/zh-cn/previous-versions/visualstudio/visual-basic-6/aa716226(v=vs.60)); [SimpleLocalize](https://simplelocalize.io/blog/posts/text-expansion-ui-localization/).
- Turkish creator context: guides stress that Reels drive discovery on Turkish Instagram — [Ensonhaber](https://www.ensonhaber.com/yapay-zeka-ile-influencer-olmak-gercekci-ai-influencer-nasil-olusturulur-h1510865). Meta's "AI info" label rules apply to AI-generated content — [SMMAIR](https://smmair.com/en/blog/instagram-yapay-zeka-icerik-uretimi-ve-araclari); [Webrazzi](https://webrazzi.com/2026/09/01/instagram-etiket-kullanmayan-yapay-zeka-hesaplarina-erisimi-kisitladi/).

### Inferences
- **Space:** leave about 20 to 30% headroom in headline boxes. Prefer auto-fit with a minimum font size, or allow a two-line headline, over fixed single-line slots.
- **Fonts:** choose fonts with full Turkish glyph coverage (ğ, ş, ı, İ, ç, ö, ü) and test them in all-caps.
- **Uppercasing:** use `toLocaleUpperCase('tr-TR')` in any HTML or JS renderer. Otherwise "istanbul" becomes "ISTANBUL", not "İSTANBUL".
- **Term choice:** keep English product names (ChatGPT, Gemini) and pair them with short Turkish verbs. Prefer "Yapay zeka" or "YZ"/"AI" depending on the audience; "AI" saves width.
- **Reach:** carousels may need reel versions for reach in Turkey.

### Gaps
- No rigorous Turkish-specific expansion study. No data on Turkish audience preferences for carousel style.
