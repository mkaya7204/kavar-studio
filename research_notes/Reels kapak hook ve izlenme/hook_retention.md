# Hook and Retention Techniques for Short-Form Vertical Video (Reels / TikTok / Shorts), 2025-2026

Scope: 15-30s Turkish AI-tool intro and AI-news Reels, synthetic (ElevenLabs) voiceover, animated text, screen recordings, no presenter. Research date 2026-10-12.

Evidence note: very little of this field is backed by peer-reviewed or platform-published data. The **strongest sources** are the platforms' own guidance: the TikTok Ads Help Center, Meta's guidance as reported by Social Media Today (Dec 2025), and YouTube Help definitions relayed by third parties. **Medium-strength sources** are vendor data posts (OpusClip, Bytecap). They usually lack methodology and come from companies that sell editing tools. **Creator-strategist frameworks** (Kallaway, Hormozi, MrBeast doc) are opinion or experience, not controlled evidence, and most of what I found about them is second-hand summaries.

## 1. Hook types (visual / spoken / text) and what works in the first 1-3 seconds

### Takeaway
The consensus, from TikTok officially and from Kallaway, is a "triple hook": on-screen text, the visual and the spoken line all say the same thing in frame one. They should state the **subject plus the viewer's outcome or stakes**, not a brand name. Platforms frame the window as 3 seconds (proposition) to 6 seconds (hook). Practitioners treat the first 1-2 seconds as decisive, but no rigorous data was found on the exact second.

### Cited Findings
- **Official (TikTok).** "Prioritize your hook in the first 6 seconds" using suspense, surprise or other emotions. "Introduce your content proposition within the first 3 seconds for better recall and awareness." Recommended structure: hook → unique selling points → clear CTA. — [TikTok Ads Help Center: Creative best practices for performance ads](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices)
- **Official (TikTok, relayed second-hand).** "Ads that use suspense early in a story lead to a +16% increase in watch time" and "surprise can lead to a 1.7x VTR". These come from TikTok's Creative Codes one-pager (dated 5.2023, so older than the 2025-26 window). I could not read the PDF text directly; the figures come via a search summary. — [TikTok Creative Codes one-pager (PDF)](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Creative_Codes_One_Pager_CA.pdf)
- **Official (Meta, Dec 17 2025).** "Great Reels nail the hook within the first few seconds." Meta names three hook types:
  - **Value promise:** the viewer's benefit up front.
  - **Statement of intent:** plainly says what the viewer will see or learn.
  - **Question / invitation:** curiosity, inviting the viewer to reflect or participate.
  Meta also recommends A/B testing hooks, since small hook changes can produce significant gains. It says younger audiences consume content about 3x faster than older ones. — [Social Media Today: Meta shares tips on Reels hooks](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-shares-tips-on-reels-hooks-creative-diversification-in-ads-and-threa/808182)
- **Kallaway, "The Psychology of Killer Hooks" (transcript, undated).** Points are listed in the order he gives them.
  - **Interrupt theory:** the hook must trigger an involuntary orienting response, which he calls the "freeze point."
  - **Two questions:** the hook must answer *what is this about* (subject) and *what do I get* (outcome).
  - **Ingredients:** subject, stakes, speed and clarity. Speed means few words, delivered fast enough to absorb. Clarity means one interpretation only, with no wordplay or vague setups.
  - **Triple hook:** text hook + visual hook + spoken hook must align. "Viewers should be able to guess one from the other two." Misalignment causes confusion and scroll-away. He calls the text hook the most important component.
  - **Visual hook categories:** graphic/text overlays, pattern interrupts / visual switching, subject motion, VFX/transitions, and visual selection.
  — [SozAI transcript of Kallaway video](https://sozai.app/transcript/psychology-killer-hooks/index.md)
- **Kallaway text-hook rules (same source):**
  - Pair a "subject repeater" (proper noun or jargon) with a pain point, a dream-outcome tease or a future state change. His example is "Claude just changed social media forever."
  - Use exact numbers ("22 days" beats "a few weeks").
  - Keep it to two lines maximum, with no punctuation except quotes or parentheses.
  - Avoid connectors ("and", "or", "that", "but").
  - Place it about one third of the way down the frame and keep it on screen for about 3 seconds.
  — [SozAI transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/psychology-killer-hooks/index.md)
- **Kallaway example opener:** "I think Google might be in trouble." This is a negative/stakes hook built around a known subject. — [Castmagic summary of podcast "Rhythm, Hooks, and a Billion Views" (Aug 2024, older)](https://www.castmagic.io/podcast/rhythm-hooks-and-a-billion-views-kane-kallaways-short-form-video-content-secrets)
- **Hormozi-attributed hook formulas (second-hand, unverified against his own material):**
  - Specific-result hook, contrarian hook, curiosity-gap hook and audience call-out hook.
  - Fill-in formulas: "[Target Audience] + [Call to Action] = [Reward]" and "WHO + RESULT + SPEED/EASE + OBJECTION REMOVAL".
  — [Solopreneur Code](https://solopreneurcode.substack.com/p/how-i-write-hooks-that-actually-work); [skillselion Hormozi hooks listing](https://skillselion.com/skills/alexsmedile/hormozi-skills/hormozi-hooks)
- Proof → Promise → Plan is Hormozi's **pitch** structure, not a hook formula. Leading with the promise before proof can trigger skepticism. — [Ampifire](https://ampifire.com/blog/alex-hormozi-proof-promise-plan-framework-to-sell-your-idea/)
- A practitioner guide recommends on-screen title text of 3-5 words in a big bold font, distilled from the spoken hook. — [Solopreneur Code](https://solopreneurcode.substack.com/p/how-i-write-hooks-that-actually-work)
- **Third-party Shorts guides:**
  - "On Shorts, your hook window is 2 seconds, not 3."
  - Common first fixes: show the subject on screen in the first frame, open with a claim or question, and cut greetings and logos at the start.
  — [blitzcutai](https://blitzcutai.com/blog/best-youtube-shorts-hooks-2026); [Buffer Shorts analytics guide](https://buffer.com/resources/the-creators-guide-to-youtube-shorts-analytics); [aituber](https://aituber.app/blog/youtube-shorts-not-getting-views/)
- **Hard-sell openings:** an agency guide says hard-sell openings make people scroll past on Reels. — [Jumpfly](https://www.jumpfly.com/blog/reel-talk-a-beginners-guide-to-metas-reels-placement/)

### Inferences
- **Why product-name-first hooks fail.** By the Kallaway "subject + outcome" rule, an unknown product name gives a subject with no stakes. "Gamma AI ile tanışın" ("Meet Gamma AI") answers neither "why should I care" nor "what do I get." It also reads as an ad, the hard-sell opening Jumpfly warns against.
  - **Exception:** a *famous* proper noun (ChatGPT, Google, Claude) works as a "subject repeater" because it carries stakes on its own. Kallaway's examples use exactly this: "Claude just changed...", "Google might be in trouble."
  - **Pipeline rule:** big-brand news can lead with the brand plus a stakes verb. Niche tools should lead with the outcome or pain, and the name comes later.
- **Weak vs strong examples for this channel** (constructed from the frameworks above, not tested):
  - Weak: "Bugün size X yapay zeka aracını tanıtacağım" ("Today I'll introduce the X AI tool"). Greeting plus unknown name, no stakes.
  - Strong, result-first: "Bu sunumu 30 saniyede yaptım" ("I made this presentation in 30 seconds"). The finished deck is on screen in frame 1.
  - Strong, negative: "PowerPoint'te saatlerini harcama" ("Don't waste hours in PowerPoint").
  - Strong, stakes + known brand: "OpenAI az önce Google'ın işini zorlaştırdı" ("OpenAI just made Google's job harder").
  - Strong, curiosity gap with a specific number: "Bu ücretsiz araç 3 uygulamanın işini yapıyor" ("This free tool does the work of 3 apps"). Avoid prices per the project's no-prices policy; "ücretsiz" (free) may also need review against that policy.
- **Triple-hook alignment for an automated pipeline:**
  - **Frame 0 visual:** the result or product output, never a logo or black screen.
  - **Text hook:** 3-6 words, two lines max, placed one third down the frame and visible from frame 0 with no slow animate-in.
  - **Spoken hook:** says the same claim, starting at frame 0 with no leading silence in the TTS file.

### Gaps
- No rigorous public study isolates hook *type* (question vs negative vs result-first) on retention. Meta's three types are guidance without comparative numbers.
- I could not access Kallaway's original framework documents. The archetype names in various "skills" listings are third-party.

## 2. Script structures for 15-30s explainers; brand placement; payoff timing; loops

### Takeaway
Use hook (0-2s) → confirm and payoff fast (by about 5-8s) → 1-2 re-hooks or proof beats → brief CTA, with the brand name after the hook has landed. Ending lines that flow back into the opening (loops) are widely recommended for replays, but the evidence is anecdotal.

### Cited Findings
- **Official TikTok structure:** hook → unique selling points → clear CTA. The proposition goes in the first 3 seconds. — [TikTok Ads Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices)
- **Kallaway "lock-in zone".** The 5-10 seconds after the hook have two jobs: confirm the hook's claim, and earn trust so it doesn't feel like bait-and-switch. Trust levers are appearance, packaging quality, relatability and proof (the "trust anchor"). — [SozAI transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/psychology-killer-hooks/index.md)
- **Kallaway rhythm.** "Set up, rehook. Set up, rehook," ending with a final hook that encourages sharing. Keep opening "curiosity loops" (a "mental itch"). Vary sentence length (short, medium, long) and speaking speed to avoid monotony. — [Castmagic podcast summary (2024)](https://www.castmagic.io/podcast/rhythm-hooks-and-a-billion-views-kane-kallaways-short-form-video-content-secrets)
- **OpusClip (vendor, no methodology).**
  - Optimal general Shorts length is 15-30s; quick tips 15-20s; tutorials 25-40s (three steps fit best).
  - The final 3 seconds should deliver the payoff, and a 10% replay rate "significantly" boosts distribution.
  - Cut script drafts by about 30%.
  - A new visual or story beat every 5-7s in storytelling formats.
  — [OpusClip: Ideal YouTube Shorts length (Nov 2025, updated Feb 2026)](https://opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention)
- **Loops:** Shorts loop back to the beginning, so the final frame can lead into the opening again. — [videobgremover Shorts best practices](https://videobgremover.com/blog/youtube-shorts-best-practices)
- **MrBeast production document** (believed written around 2022, leaked Aug 2024; authenticity confirmed by two ex-producers to Passionfruit, MrBeast declined comment; long-form focus, so it is older and only an analogy):
  - The key metrics are CTR, AVD and AVP.
  - The opening is where most viewers leave; the doc cites a loss of 21M viewers in the first minute of one video.
  - "Re-engagement" content is inserted at intervals (around the 3 and 6 minute marks in long-form).
  - If the packaging promises something, the video must deliver it.
  — [Passionfruit](https://passionfru.it/mrbeast-leaked-document-81151/); [Netinfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/mrbeast-productions-leaked-internal-document-rosanna-pansino/); [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/leaked-document-allegedly-reveals-mrbeasts-secrets-to-youtube-success-the-key-takeaways/)

### Inferences
- **Template for a 20-25s AI-tool Reel:**

  | Time | Beat | What happens |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0-2s | Hook | Result or pain, spoken + text + visual aligned |
  | 2-6s | Confirm | Show the output or screen recording proving the claim; this is the "lock-in" |
  | 6-8s | Name | Tool name introduced naturally ("Adı X") |
  | 8-18s | Proof | 2-3 fast "what it does" beats, each with a new visual every 2-4s and a mini re-hook ("ama asıl olay şu", "but here's the real thing") |
  | 18-23s | Twist / CTA | Twist or best feature, then a short CTA (save/follow/comment keyword) |
  | Last line | Loop | Optionally phrased so it flows back into the first line |

- **AI-news structure:**
  1. Stakes headline (known brand + verb).
  2. What happened, in one sentence, with a screenshot.
  3. Why it matters to the viewer.
  4. One surprising detail (re-hook).
  5. Question CTA.
- **MrBeast analogy:** "deliver what the cover/hook promises" applies directly. If the Reel cover says "X yapıyor" ("X does this"), show X doing it by about 5s.

### Gaps
- No primary data quantifies loop endings' effect on Reels distribution. Replay value is asserted, not measured publicly.
- I found no primary Paddy Galloway material on Shorts structure. One source only characterizes him as "data-first retention principles" ([outlierkit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-scriptwriting-methods-compared/)).

## 3. Editing: cut frequency, zooms, B-roll, captions, safe zones, sound, voice WPM, dead air

### Takeaway
Change something visually every 2-4s, cut all silences, and burn in captions/text that work muted. Keep everything inside the Reels safe zone (about 14% top, 35% bottom, 6% sides). Use music or voiceover; Meta reports a conversion lift. Narration around 150-180 English WPM is the convention. Turkish needs a syllable-based check.

### Cited Findings
- **Cut frequency (vendor, unsourced):** high-performing Shorts average one cut every 2-4 seconds. — [OpusClip](https://opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention)
- **Remove dead air (vendor):** remove every pause and filler word, and speed up action footage 1.2-1.5x. Cutting the first 5 seconds reportedly doubled one creator's retention (anecdote). — [OpusClip](https://opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention)
- **Text density (official TikTok):** "5-10 words per second when using text." TikTok also recommends captions/text overlays for context, plus transitions, stickers and graphics to retain view time. — [TikTok Ads Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices)
  - Note: 5-10 words/second is very high. It likely refers to caption-reading capacity, not a requirement to fill the screen.
- **Captions (vendor, no baseline or method):** "Shorts with burned-in captions see 15-25% higher retention than those without." — [OpusClip](https://opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention)
- **Kallaway on the text hook:** distinct from captions, placed about one third down the frame, on screen about 3s. — [SozAI](https://sozai.app/transcript/psychology-killer-hooks/index.md)
- **Visual hooks:** start with small moves (moving text, a lean-in) rather than gimmicks. Motion, color and contrast create the "stun gun" effect of visual switching. — [SozAI](https://sozai.app/transcript/psychology-killer-hooks/index.md)
- **Safe zones (agency guides, consistent with Meta's Ads Manager "Safe Zone Guardrail"):**
  - Reels: keep top 14%, bottom 35% and 6% on each side clear. On a 1440×2560 frame that is about 358px top, 896px bottom and 86px sides.
  - One guide claims Stories and Reels moved to a unified safe zone in March 2026; this is unverified.
  — [Adstellar vertical ad best practices](https://www.adstellar.ai/blog/vertical-video-ad-best-practices); [Billo: Meta safe zones 2026](https://billo.app/?p=50654); [Adnova](https://adnova.ai/blogs/meta-ad-safe-zones-guide)
- **TikTok safe zone:** keep main creative elements central to avoid UI. Pixel values vary by guide (for example, the bottom ~150px). — [House of Marketers](https://www.houseofmarketers.com/guide-to-safe-zones-tiktok-facebook-instagram-stories)
- **Sound (official Meta, Dec 2025):** audio is "the hidden gem." Reels campaigns with music or voiceover delivered "up to 13% higher incremental conversions." — [Social Media Today](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-shares-tips-on-reels-hooks-creative-diversification-in-ads-and-threa/808182)
- **Muted viewing (vendor):** a vendor claims 70-80% of Reels viewers have audio on (unverified). The best Reels still work without sound. — [benly.ai](https://benly.ai/learn/meta-ads/meta-ads-reels-ads-guide)
  - A Kallaway-adjacent guide says most viewers start with sound off, so on-screen text does the work. — [kompozy](https://kompozy.io/guides/short-form-video-hooks)
  - These two claims **conflict**. Design for both.
- **Voice pacing (voiceover-industry guide, English):**

  | Delivery style | Pace |
  |---|---|
  | Conversational narration | 130-160 WPM |
  | Casual social video | 150-165 WPM |
  | High-energy promo | 165-180 WPM |
  | Risk of listening fatigue and drop-off | above 180 WPM |
  | Tends to lose viewers | below 120 WPM |

  — [Syllaby](https://syllaby.io/blog/voiceover-pacing-silence-trimming-retention-editing/). Most creators initially speak too slowly; aim for "articulate rather than chipmunk-fast" — [SocialKit](https://socialk.it/en/blog/voiceover-videos-for-social-media)
- **Platform pace:** match platform pace. Slow podcast-style clips underperform on TikTok and IG. — [Castmagic/Kallaway](https://www.castmagic.io/podcast/rhythm-hooks-and-a-billion-views-kane-kallaways-short-form-video-content-secrets)

### Inferences
- **Concrete pipeline rules:**
  1. The TTS file starts at 0 ms. Trim leading silence and cap any internal pause at about 150-250 ms.
  2. The hook text is fully visible by frame 0-6 (≤0.2s). Avoid typewriter or slow fade-ins over 300 ms on the hook.
  3. Change the visual every 2-3s: zoom-in on a screen recording, cut, new screenshot, highlight box or cursor emphasis.
  4. Use word-by-word or phrase captions in the center band, above the bottom 35%. Keep the hook title around 30-35% from the top. Never put text in the top 14% or the right 6% (the like/comment rail).
  5. Keep a low music bed under the voice, ducked. Add whoosh/pop SFX on cuts as optional pattern interrupts.
- **Turkish WPM:** Turkish is agglutinative and words are longer, so English WPM targets overstate Turkish word counts.
  - A practical Turkish proxy is about 4.5-5.5 syllables per second, or about 120-150 Turkish words per minute. This is my inference and has not been sourced.
  - Measure WPM of ElevenLabs output per voice and test speed 1.05-1.15x against retention.
  - A 25s Reel at about 140 Turkish WPM is roughly 55-60 words of script.

### Gaps
- No platform-published cut-frequency or caption-style experiment was found. The 2-4s and +15-25% figures are vendor claims.
- No Turkish-specific narration-speed research was found.
- I did not verify Meta's official Help Center safe-zone page directly.

## 4. Faceless channels and synthetic voice

### Takeaway
No controlled study shows AI voice per se hurts retention. Practitioner consensus is that *low-quality, templated* AI narration over generic footage underperforms. YouTube's July 2025 "inauthentic content" policy targets mass-produced, templated sameness, not AI voice itself.

### Cited Findings
- **Retention claims:** channels using AI narration over recycled stock footage reportedly show lower retention than channels with real research and a distinctive voice. This claim comes without data. Poor-quality AI voiceovers can reduce retention. — [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/ai-faceless-channel-monetization-youtube-policy); [Resemble AI](https://www.resemble.ai/resources/ai-voice-youtube-videos-monetize)
- **YouTube policy (July 2025):** YPP renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content." It targets templated, mass-produced, easily replicable content with little variation; voice type is not the test. Realistic synthetic content must be disclosed. — [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/ai-faceless-channel-monetization-youtube-policy); [Medium (Starcevic Filipovic)](https://mateostarcevicfilipovic.medium.com/youtube-quietly-demonetizes-faceless-ai-channels-since-july-2025-30b3d2daaf2e); [InVideo](https://invideo.io/blog/youtube-kills-ai-faceless-channels/)
  - These are secondary sources, and some vendors have an interest in the topic.
- **Vendor claim:** custom narrator voices give a 15-25% VTR lift (no study cited). — [Narration Box](https://narrationbox.com/blog/how-tiktok-creators-use-ai-narrators-to-go-viral-%282025-playbook%29)
- **TikTok on people:** TikTok recommends featuring people (creators, employees, customers) to capture attention and increase engagement. A pure faceless format forgoes this lever. — [TikTok Ads Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices)
- **Kallaway's own case:** he is a tech/AI creator with over 550K followers who presents on camera. His hook rules (text hook, visual switching, subject repeater) are format-agnostic. — [Castmagic](https://www.castmagic.io/podcast/rhythm-hooks-and-a-billion-views-kane-kallaways-short-form-video-content-secrets); [SozAI](https://sozai.app/transcript/psychology-killer-hooks/index.md)

### Inferences
- **Faceless retention levers:** without a face, the visual hook must come from:
  - motion (zooms and cursor moves on screen recordings);
  - result reveals (before/after);
  - high-contrast text hooks;
  - visual switching every 2-3s.
- **Making the voice feel human:** pick an expressive ElevenLabs voice with varied sentence lengths (Kallaway's rhythm rule), use conversational Turkish ("bak", "şunu gör", "inanılmaz değil mi"), and avoid a monotone news-anchor read.
- **Inauthentic-content risk:** to reduce it, vary templates (layouts, hook styles, color accents) across posts. Each video should add original curation or opinion, not just a reworded press release.
- **Optional:** a recurring hand/cursor or a persona "voice" (the same ElevenLabs voice and catchphrases) could partially substitute for a face as a "trust anchor" (untested).

### Gaps
- No A/B data comparing ElevenLabs-class voices with human voice on Reels retention was found.
- No Instagram-specific policy equivalent to YouTube's inauthentic-content rule was researched here.

## 5. Native, creator-made feel vs ad feel

### Takeaway
TikTok officially recommends a DIY, not-overly-polished UGC aesthetic, trends/memes and people on camera. Meta recommends value-promise and question hooks and fast iteration. Agency guidance says hard-sell openings get scrolled past.

### Cited Findings
- **Official TikTok:** "Use a DIY or not overly polished aesthetic that fits with user-generated content". Also lean into trends, memes or challenges, and use transitions, stickers and graphics. — [TikTok Ads Help Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices)
- **TikTok support pages:** aim for a casual, user-generated style. — [support.ui.marketing summary](https://support.ui.marketing/tiktok-creative-best-practices); [TikTok Creative Guidance](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/quicktok/online/tiktok_creative_accelerator/pc/en)
- **Official Meta:** value-promise, statement-of-intent and question/invitation hooks, with A/B testing; music or voiceover helps. — [Social Media Today (Dec 2025)](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-shares-tips-on-reels-hooks-creative-diversification-in-ads-and-threa/808182)
- **Agency guidance:** content that blends in with organic Reels works better, and hard-sell openings get skipped. — [Jumpfly](https://www.jumpfly.com/blog/reel-talk-a-beginners-guide-to-metas-reels-placement/)
- **Platform pace:** native pace matters; slow content underperforms on TikTok and IG. — [Castmagic/Kallaway](https://www.castmagic.io/podcast/rhythm-hooks-and-a-billion-views-kane-kallaways-short-form-video-content-secrets)

### Inferences
- **Native-feel checklist for this pipeline:**
  - No intro logo or brand bumper.
  - First person, talking-to-a-friend Turkish ("Bunu denedim", "I tried this").
  - Show real screen recordings rather than glossy mockups.
  - Use one or two meme/sticker-style emphasis graphics.
  - Keep the CTA conversational ("kaydet, lazım olacak", "save it, you'll need it") rather than a sales line.
- **Collaborations:** for collaborations, the project's disclosure policy still applies. Native style does not mean hiding sponsorship.

### Gaps
- The TikTok Creative Codes PDF (2023) could not be text-extracted, so I could not verify its native/UGC statistics.
- The widely repeated "first 6 seconds = 90% of ad recall" TikTok statistic could not be found on official pages and is unverified.

## 6. Retention benchmarks (3-second hold, average watch %)

### Takeaway
There are no official universal benchmarks; all platforms and the more careful analysts say to benchmark against your own channel. These vendor heuristics are commonly cited and can serve as rough targets:

| Metric | Target | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 3-second hold / "stayed to watch" | above 70-80% | Shorts vendors |
| Average % viewed for 15-30s Shorts | at least 60%; 70%+ "excellent" | Shorts vendors |
| Meta hook rate (3s views ÷ impressions) | above 30% | ads vendor |

### Cited Findings
- **Drop-off timing (OpusClip, vendor, no methodology):**
  - "50-60% of viewers who drop off do so within the first three seconds."
  - Intro retention (past 3s) should be above 70%.
  - Completion above 60% for Shorts under 30s.
  - Average retention of at least 60%, with 70%+ excellent and top performers above 80%.
  — [OpusClip](https://opus.pro/blog/ideal-youtube-shorts-length-format-retention)
- **No official threshold (Bytecap, vendor evidence synthesis, updated July 2026):** YouTube publishes no universal "good retention" threshold. Benchmark against your own Shorts of similar duration, format, topic and traffic source. Since March 2025, public Shorts views count starts or replays with no minimum watch time. — [Bytecap](https://www.bytecap.io/research/youtube-shorts-retention-benchmarks)
- **YouTube metric:** "Viewed vs swiped away" (now "Stayed to watch") measures whether viewers stayed past the opening seconds. It does not measure completion. One guide suggests a 75-80% viewed target but notes no official threshold. A low share means the first ~2 seconds did not earn the watch. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/the-creators-guide-to-youtube-shorts-analytics); [ContentStudio](https://contentstudio.io/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics-guide); [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-analytics-metrics-growth)
- **Meta hook rate (vendor):** Meta Hook Rate (3-second views ÷ impressions) as a quality signal, aiming above 30%. This threshold is not verified in Meta docs. — [benly.ai](https://benly.ai/learn/meta-ads/video-ads-guide)
- **Average Reel watch time (Metricool, via Influencer Marketing Hub):** rose from about 4 seconds (2025) to 8.5 seconds (2026). Metricool earlier cited a 3.75s average TikTok watch time. — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://www.influencermarketinghub.com/blog/instagram-reels-statistics); [Metricool newsletter](https://newsletter.metricool.com/p/8-ideas-for-your-short-videos-and-a-100-meter-world-record)
- **Socialinsider 2025 IG benchmarks (data Jan 2023-Dec 2024):**
  - Reels average 0.50% engagement per post.
  - Accounts under 5K followers see a 20% view rate on Reels and about 300 average views.
  - Accounts over 100K followers get about 15K views per Reel.
  — [Socialinsider Instagram benchmarks 2025 (PDF)](https://www.socialinsider.io/data-geeks/instagram_benchmarks_2025.pdf)
- **Quso (self-reported, own platform):** top creators hold 73% average retention vs 12% for the bottom decile. — [Quso](https://www.quso.ai/tools/video-hook-generator)

### Inferences
- **Instagram Insights:** use per-Reel metrics such as average watch time, watch time and (where available) skip rate. Track per hook type in the pipeline's logs to run your own Meta-recommended hook A/B tests.
- **Starting targets for a 20-25s Reel:**
  - First goal: a 3s hold of 65-70%+ and an average watch of 50-60%+.
  - Iterate on the hook first, since that is where most drop-off occurs.
